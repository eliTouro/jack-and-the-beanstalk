Privacy policy

This site turns one photo of a child into a Jack and the Beanstalk picture book. It's a student project for an AI Engineering class at Touro University. Here's what it keeps and why.

When you sign in with Google

The site receives your name, email address, profile picture and Google account ID. It uses them only to keep your books in your own account and show your name at the top of the page. It asks Google for nothing else: no contacts, no Drive, no Gmail.

What you make

  • The photo you upload, the pictures made from it, and what you type on the About me page are saved to your account, so the book is still there when you come back.
  • Only you can see them. Every picture is checked against the signed-in account before it's sent.
  • To draw the pictures, the photo is sent to OpenAI's image API. OpenAI says it doesn't use data sent through its API to train its models.

Logs

The server records each request (page, time, status) and events such as "signed in" or "picture finished in 14 seconds", to fix problems and estimate how long pictures take. Cookies and photos are never written to the logs.

Cookies

One cookie keeps you signed in for up to 30 days. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking cookies. Nothing is sold or shared with anyone else.

Deleting your data

Deleting a book on the site deletes its photo, pictures and text straight away. To have your whole account removed, open an issue on the project's GitHub page and it will be deleted.

Where it's stored

The site runs on Render and stores data in a Postgres database hosted by Neon, both in the United States.
